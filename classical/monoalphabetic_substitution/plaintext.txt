2.13 Prove an analogue of Theorem 2.7 for the case of almost perfect secrecy. That is, let epsilon < 1 be a constant and say we only require that for any distribution over M, any m in M, and any c in C,

|Pr[M = m | C = c] - Pr[M = m]| < epsilon.

Prove a lower bound on the size of the key space K relative to M for any encryption scheme that meets this definition.

Hint: Consider the uniform distribution over M and fix a ciphertext c. Then show that for a (1 - epsilon) fraction of the messages m in M, there must exist a key mapping m to c.
